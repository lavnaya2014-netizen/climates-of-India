Welcome to the World of Climate!

India is a country with great climatic diversity. From the snowy Himalayas to the hot deserts of Rajasthan and the rainy regions of Meghalaya, different parts of India experience different climatic conditions.

Did You Know?

India is mainly known for its tropical monsoon climate. The monsoon winds play an important role in India's rainfall and agriculture.

1. Climate, Season and Weather

These three terms are related to atmospheric conditions, but they have different meanings.

🌦️ Weather

Weather is the day-to-day condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time. It includes temperature, rainfall, humidity and wind.

Example: A rainy afternoon in Delhi.

🌍 Climate

Climate is the average pattern of weather conditions of a place over a long period, usually 30 years or more.

Example: Rajasthan generally has a hot and dry climate.

☀️ Season

A season is a part of the year that has particular weather characteristics. Seasons change during the year.

Example: Summer, winter and rainy season.

2. Types of Climate in India

India has different climatic regions because of its geographical features and location.

🏜️ Hot and Dry Climate

Found in parts of Rajasthan and Gujarat. These areas receive little rainfall and often experience very high temperatures.

🌧️ Tropical Wet Climate

Found in parts of the Western Ghats and Northeast India. These regions receive heavy rainfall.

🌳 Tropical Wet and Dry Climate

Found in many parts of central and southern India. These areas have distinct wet and dry seasons.

❄️ Mountain Climate

Found in the Himalayan region. Temperature generally decreases with increasing altitude, and higher areas may receive snowfall.

🌤️ Humid Subtropical Climate

Found in parts of northern India. Summers are generally hot, winters are cooler, and rainfall occurs mainly during the monsoon.

Remember: India is mainly described as having a tropical monsoon climate, although climatic conditions differ from region to region.

3. Factors Determining Climate

Several geographical factors affect the climate of a place.

📍 1. Latitude

Latitude determines how directly the Sun's rays fall on a place. Places closer to the Equator generally receive more direct sunlight.

⛰️ 2. Altitude

Altitude means height above sea level. Higher places are generally cooler than lower places.

🌊 3. Distance from the Sea

Coastal areas usually have moderate temperatures, while places far from the sea experience greater temperature differences.

🌬️ 4. Winds

Winds carry heat and moisture from one place to another. Moisture-bearing monsoon winds bring rainfall to India.

🏔️ 5. Relief Features

Mountains affect the movement of winds and rainfall. The Himalayas block cold winds from Central Asia and influence India's climate.

🌡️ 6. Pressure and Ocean Currents

Air pressure systems influence wind direction and rainfall. Ocean currents can also affect the temperature of nearby coastal regions.

Example: Mumbai and Nagpur

Mumbai is near the sea, so it experiences a moderate climate. Nagpur is far from the sea, so it experiences greater temperature variations.

4. The Monsoon

The word monsoon refers to the seasonal reversal of winds. Monsoon winds are extremely important for India's climate because they bring most of the country's annual rainfall.

🌧️ Southwest Monsoon

Usually occurs from June to September. Moisture-laden winds blow from the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal towards India, bringing widespread rainfall.

🌬️ Northeast Monsoon

Usually occurs from October to December. These winds generally blow from land towards the sea. However, after crossing the Bay of Bengal, they bring rainfall to parts of southeastern India, especially Tamil Nadu.

🌾 Importance of Monsoon

Monsoon rainfall helps agriculture, fills rivers, lakes and reservoirs, and provides water for people, animals and plants.

Four Main Seasons of India

  1. Cold Weather Season – December to February
  2. Hot Weather Season – March to May
  3. Southwest Monsoon Season – June to September
  4. Retreating Monsoon Season – October to November

These are the four broad seasons commonly recognised in India's climate.
